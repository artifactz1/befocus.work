BeFocus - Phase 2 - Customization Engine and Panel

Where should the customize panel live?

Phase 2 builds the panel that changes every customizable surface with live preview and Apply / Cancel. The controls, their order and the preview-then-commit flow are already decided. What's still open is the shape of the panel on desktop and mobile. This board compares four working directions on a replica of the real dashboard. D, which restyles A after the reference you shared, is recommended.

Decided 2026-09-27 The owner picked D - Feathered dock. It is recorded as D-20 in the Phase 2 CONTEXT and specified in 02-UI-SPEC.md.

Your reference: the AI Companion sidebar

From the post you shared (an AI sidebar by @gabriell_lab, via bestdesignsonx). Frames pulled from the 7-second clip, before and after the sidebar opens. Direction D below applies this language to the customize panel.

What makes it work

  1. No edge. The sidebar has no border, radius or shadow. It's the same material as the canvas, one step lighter. Space is split by light, not lines.
  2. A feathered seam. Over about 120px, canvas content fades into the sidebar surface and blurs as it goes. It reads as depth instead of a cut.
  3. Soft fills, one lift. Groups sit on a faint tint with 16-20px radii, and rows get a hairline. One element per view, the result card, is lifted with a lighter fill and a soft shadow, so the eye knows where to land.
  4. Staged motion. Content un-blurs into place, then items stagger in from the top. A one-time shimmer on "ready" marks the state change.
  5. Quiet type. One family at 13-15px, muted second lines, truncated descriptions and generous gaps. Numbered keycaps on each row double as shortcuts.
  6. Anchored trigger. The sidebar hangs under its top-bar chip, and the chip fills while the sidebar is open.

What D carries over

  • The feathered left edge with a blur ramp. The controls still sit on an opaque surface.
  • A panel surface made from your background color plus 3.5% light, so the panel is the same material as the dashboard and follows the look as you preview.
  • Soft-filled control groups, pill section chips, and one lifted element (the selected card or the current look).
  • A blur-in stagger when opening or switching sections, and a one-shot ping on the "Previewing changes" dot. Both turn off under reduced motion.

What it leaves out

  • Light theme: BeFocus is dark only.
  • Top-bar trigger: the footer palette button is the locked entry point. It fills with the accent while open, echoing the chip.
  • Text shimmer: replaced by the dot ping, because animated text competes with the timer.
  • Numbered shortcuts: optional later (1-5 jump between sections while the panel is open), left out of the demo.

Four directions

Same controls and the same preview engine in all four. Only the container, its surface, the timer's motion and where Apply / Cancel sit are different.

D

Feathered dock

ChosenFrom your reference

A's layout in the language of your reference. The panel docks right with no border. The dashboard fades and blurs into it over 120px, and its surface is your background color one step lighter, so it reads as part of the dashboard instead of a card on top. The timer never leaves the center. It only scales down until it clears the feather. On mobile the top edge of the sheet feathers the same way.

Desktop 1440x900. Panel 520px, of which 120px is feather and 372px is controls. Timer stays centered at scale(.42), and the key hints hide while the panel is open.Switch sections to see the blur-in. Pick another background to see the panel follow.
Sheet at 60%, with the top 34px feathered and no scrim.

Live preview

  • The panel surface follows the previewed background, so picking Moss or Plum re-tints the panel too. The preview feels continuous instead of framed.
  • The timer stays at the center of the screen and only scales, so the composition you edit is the one you get after Apply.

Apply / Cancel

  • Same as A: Reset on the left, Cancel and Apply on the right, in a footer that fades the scrolling content out instead of drawing a line.
  • The "Previewing changes" dot pings once when the first change lands.

Why it wins

  • A's room for Phases 4-6 and the prototype's shape, with a centered composition like B and C.
  • Calmer: no hard edge and one lifted element, and the panel belongs to your look instead of sitting on it. That suits a focus app.
  • Built from the same parts as A, so the build cost is about the same.

What it costs

  • Smaller timer while editing: centered, it has to clear the panel from the middle, so it drops to 42% at 1440 (digits about 118px tall). The timer is sized by viewport height and the free space grows with width, so a 1920x1080 screen already gets the full 72%.
  • backdrop-filter over a moving timer costs GPU on low-end laptops. The fallback is the gradient alone, which still reads well.
  • With image and video backgrounds (Phase 5), the surface has to come from a neutral dark tone instead of the background color. It's planned for, not a redesign.
  • The wordmark and menu still sit under the panel, as in A.
A

Docked side panel

A full-height solid panel docks on the right. The timer slides into the free space and scales to 72%, so the whole background, accent and type stay visible at full bleed while you edit. On mobile the same sections sit in a bottom sheet and the timer rises above it. This is the shape already walked through in the customization prototype.

Desktop 1440x900. Panel 400px wide, inset 16px. Timer translateX(-208px) scale(.72).Close the panel to see the timer glide back.
Sheet at 56% height with no scrim, so the preview stays visible.

Live preview

  • Background, grain, accent and type repaint the whole viewport behind the panel.
  • The timer shrinks to 72% and shifts left. Its composition is not 1:1 while editing, but it snaps back on close.
  • Header and footer controls stay where they are. The panel covers the wordmark and menu while it's open.

Apply / Cancel

  • Sticky footer: Reset on the left, Cancel and Apply on the right.
  • Apply stays disabled until something changes. The header shows "Previewing changes" with an accent dot.
  • Close, Esc and Cancel all revert. Apply commits and closes.

Strengths

  • Most room: sections scroll vertically, so Phase 4 themes, Phase 5 uploads and quota, and Phase 6 URL input append without a redesign. The roadmap requires that Phase 4 "fills rather than restructures".
  • The timer keeps the most size of any option (72%).
  • Desktop and mobile share one section stack. Only the shell differs.
  • The owner has already seen and accepted this shape in the prototype.

What it costs

  • The composition sits off-center while editing, so a density preview is read on a shifted layout.
  • Two shells: a desktop aside plus a mobile Drawer. packages/ui has drawer (vaul) but no sheet, so the desktop aside is new.
  • Covers the wordmark and menu while open. That's fine, since the menu is the entry point.
B

Bottom tray

A full-width tray slides up over the footer. The sections become a left rail, and the controls lay out in columns. The timer stays centered and scales to 62% above the tray, so the composition keeps its symmetry. On mobile the same tray becomes the sheet, which means one shell for both.

Desktop 1440x900. Tray 330px tall. Timer translateY(-78px) scale(.62). Footer hidden while open.Apply / Cancel sit in the tray header.
Sheet at 50%, with actions in the sheet header and a 2-column body.

Live preview

  • The layout stays centered, so it's closer to what you'll get once the panel closes.
  • The timer drops to 62%, and the transport pill is hidden under the tray (Space still works).

Apply / Cancel

  • Tray header, right: Reset, Cancel, Apply. No close button. Cancel is the close.
  • The actions sit next to the section rail, so they're always in view without a footer.

Strengths

  • Symmetric composition while editing.
  • One shell for desktop and mobile.
  • Feels like a distinct "edit mode".

What it costs

  • The fixed 330px height forces controls into columns. Font cards, sliders and the future upload dropzone, quota meter and URL warning all get cramped, and Phase 5 / 6 would likely need a taller tray, which would be a restructure.
  • The timer is smaller (62%), and the footer controls disappear while editing.
  • A 2-column mobile body squeezes the font cards and sliders.
C

Floating card

A compact card floats centered above the footer, following the April spec's "inline panel above the pill". Its height follows the active section: slim for Color and Style, taller for Background, Type and Theme. The timer rises and rescales to fit above it. On mobile it's a sheet with three snap points: peek, half and full.

Desktop 1440x900. Card 460px wide, 330-440px tall by section. Timer scale 0.8 to 0.42.Switch tabs to see the timer resize.
Snap points peek 34%, half 56% and full 90%. Use the chips or tap the handle.

Live preview

  • The footer transport and menu stay visible and usable. The timer only moves vertically.
  • Switching sections changes the card's height, and the timer rescales each time (0.8 to 0.42). That's motion noise while comparing looks.

Apply / Cancel

  • Card footer: Reset on the left, Cancel and Apply on the right, the same order as A.
  • At the full snap point on mobile the timer is hidden, so you preview by dropping back to peek.

Strengths

  • The lightest touch. It reads like a popover from the menu.
  • The chrome stays usable, so you can start a session mid-edit.
  • A peek snap point gives the biggest mobile preview of any option.

What it costs

  • Tall sections (Theme, and later uploads) squeeze the timer to about 42%.
  • Variable height, plus three snap points on mobile, adds the most motion and gesture complexity. It's also the hardest to keep UX-05 true at 1024x640.
  • The card sits between two footer groups, so its anchor to the palette button is weak.
